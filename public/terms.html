Terms

Scarab keeps your household's finances in a vault only you can open. The server stores ciphertext. You hold the keys, and there is no reset.

You signed in with Google to read this: Identity-Aware Proxy sits in front of everything on scarab.one, this page included. The same text lives in the public repository — PRIVACY.md, LICENSE and this file — for anyone who wants to read it before signing in.

What Scarab is

Scarab is a household finance app: cash and budget, investments and tax lots, real estate, a home-purchase plan, a Monte Carlo future. On scarab.one it runs entirely in your browser tab. You import statements, the tab parses them, and every number you see is computed there.

The server does three things. It stores your vault, one encrypted blob it cannot read, with the earlier versions it keeps. It publishes a daily price basket, the same file for everyone. And it relays invitations between households. It answers nothing else; the complete list is one short file, server/zk-routes.ts.

Your account and your keys

You sign in with a Google account. Google's Identity-Aware Proxy checks it before any request reaches Scarab, so Scarab has no passwords and no sign-up form of its own. Signing in and creating a vault is the whole account.

Your vault is encrypted in your browser under a key that only your passkeys and your recovery code can unwrap. There is no password reset. Lose every passkey and the recovery code, and nobody can open the vault, the operator included. The data is gone. Keep the recovery code on paper. In a household, the other member's passkey is the practical recovery: they unlock and add you again.

Households and invitations

Anyone signed in can invite any email address into their household. Nobody joins without saying yes: the invitation waits at the front door, shows who sent it, and can be declined, which tells the sender only that it is gone. Joining means everyone in that household can read what you add, and it ends the vault you had of your own. A member can leave at any time. The owner can remove a member, which re-keys the vault.

What the service holds, and its limits

One vault per household, up to 10 MB encrypted. The server keeps a bounded history of earlier versions and prunes the rest; a re-key deletes the history. Request bodies are capped. The privacy page says exactly what the server stores and what it can see anyway: your email and when you save, sizes, invitations, basket fetches.

How to check what runs here

Every deploy is a tagged release built by GitHub's runners from a public commit, twice, on two machines, and accepted only when both builds match byte for byte. Sigstore attests the image and the manifest of every served file. The operator deploys by image digest after verifying that attestation, and the server sends a Content-Security-Policy that lets the page load and talk to this origin only. Anyone can check that scarab.one serves the files of the release it names: the commands are in PRIVACY.md, “The trust root” and in scripts/verify-served.sh. The commit served to you is shown in the app under Data & Vault → Advanced → Served code.

The service may end

scarab.one is run by one person at their own cost. It may be shut down, with notice at the front door ahead of time. Your data is yours to take out at any moment, not only then: Data & Vault → Backups exports everything as one readable JSON file, and makes an encrypted .scarab backup that opens with your passkey or recovery code on any Scarab, including one you run yourself.
